Secure by design — Enterprise Data Protection (EDP)

Copilot doesn't bypass your security — it rides on top of it. Here's what's protecting you and where your data goes.

What Enterprise Data Protection gives you

On by default for anyone signed in with a work account.
  • Your data isn't used to train models. Your prompts, Copilot's responses, and the data it retrieves for grounding are never used to train the foundation models.
  • It inherits your existing protections. Copilot applies your organization's Microsoft 365 identity, security, compliance and privacy policies — including Microsoft Purview and sensitivity labels.
  • It respects permissions. Copilot can only surface content you already have access to — it never widens your reach.
  • Your data stays in the service boundary. Models are hosted in the Microsoft Cloud, within the Microsoft 365 service boundary; OpenAI has no access to your data or the model.

How a prompt flows

Every request is encrypted end-to-end.
1 · You prompt Copilot

From Chat or inside an app.

2 · Grounding via Microsoft Graph

Retrieves only content you're allowed to see, and/or the web.

3 · Responsible AI check

Prompt and results pass automated RAI checks.

4 · LLM in the Microsoft Cloud

Processed in the service boundary — not used for training.

5 · Compliance & Purview

Responses honor your data-protection policies.

6 · Response back to you

Returned with citations to its sources.

Nothing new to configure. Copilot automatically inherits the security, compliance and privacy controls your organization already set in Microsoft 365.

Terms you'll hear a lot

A few names sound alike. Here's how to tell them apart.

Chat Basic & Premium

The default Copilot chat experience — the prompt box you type into. It's the same chat surface whether you're on Copilot Chat (Basic) or M365 Copilot (Premium).

Word / Excel / PowerPoint Agents Premium

Task-focused agents built for a specific app, opened through M365 Copilot or Teams. Not the same as "Edit with Copilot." On Basic: these agents need a Premium license, but Copilot Chat (Basic) users can still have files created for them via code interpreter triggered from the prompt — Word, Excel, PowerPoint and PDF, returned as downloads.

Edit with Copilot Premium

Assisted editing inside the file you have open — draft, rewrite or summarize what's in front of you (the ribbon button and side pane from Module 1). It can take a multi-step pass at your document, workbook or deck — the mode once called "Agent Mode."

Don't mix these up: Edit with Copilot (assisted editing in the file you have open) is different from the Word / Excel / PowerPoint Agents (app-specific agents you open from Copilot or Teams).

More words you'll hear
Work IQ Premium

The intelligence layer that personalizes Copilot to you and your organization, grounding it in your work data.

Grounding

Giving Copilot trusted sources — the web, your files, or your work data through Work IQ — to base an answer on.

Agent

A customized version of Copilot scoped to specific instructions and knowledge for a task or role.

Agent Builder via M365 Copilot

The simple, no-code way to build your own agents directly inside Microsoft 365 Copilot (covered in Module 6).

Copilot Studio

The platform for building more sophisticated agents and workflows, with connectors to the apps your company uses.

Copilot Pages Basic & Premium

A persistent, shareable canvas for working on content together with Copilot and your team.

Notebooks Basic & Premium

A project-specific space where you group content so Copilot grounds its answers in just that material. Open it from the Copilot app (Notebooks) or from OneNote. The account also needs a SharePoint or OneDrive service plan.

Create Basic & Premium

Copilot's AI design studio for generating branded images, video and graphics.

Researcher & Analyst Premium

Advanced reasoning agents — Researcher for deep, multi-step research; Analyst for data analysis.

Enterprise Data Protection (EDP) Basic & Premium

The security and privacy commitments that keep your prompts and data protected in Copilot (see the section above).

Work IQ on/off — web-grounded vs. Work IQ–grounded chat

There aren't two separate chats anymore. There's one chat box with a single Work IQ toggle — flip it to control what Copilot grounds its answer in.

Work IQ off

Web-grounded Chat

Copilot reasons over public web knowledge and the files you hand it — ideal for research, drafting and general questions. Every prompt starts with you finding, attaching or pasting the material first.

  • Open-web knowledge
  • Plus any files you attach
  • Available to Basic & Premium
Work IQ on

Work IQ–grounded Chat

Copilot also reasons over your work across Microsoft 365 — emails, chats, meetings and files — trimmed to what you already have permission to see. The context is already there before you type.

  • Everything web-grounded does
  • Your emails, chats, meetings and files
  • Permission-trimmed — nothing you can't already open

Without Work IQReview /Document A and compare it against /Document B.

With Work IQList my meetings today and the key decisions I should make.

Notice the pattern. Both prompts begin with you supplying the raw material — the documents, the pasted text. Copilot is capable, but it starts from zero every time.

The prompt gets shorter, and the question gets bigger. You stop describing the material and start asking about your actual work.

From vague to valuable

The same task — preparing for an AI Engineer interview — gets stronger as you add ingredients.

Good

Better

Seven prompting best practices
1

Length & clarity matter

Too short gives generic answers; too long and Copilot may miss instructions. Be clear and complete.

2

Break it into steps

Outline first, then expand each section, then add statistics and detail.

3

Use a persona

Tell Copilot the role to adopt for expert, tailored responses.

4

Ask what it needs

Not sure what to include? Ask Copilot what details it needs to succeed.

5

Think holistically

Look at whole processes — risk, comms, customer experience — not just single tasks.

6

Be curious

Experiment with different structures. Curiosity reveals new capabilities.

7

Test & refine

Iterate. Adjust prompts based on the results you get.

Fun fact! — ask for the output format you want

Copilot doesn't only answer in chat — you can ask it to deliver the result as a ready-to-edit file in the format you need. Just name the format in your prompt and Copilot builds the document, deck, workbook or page for you.

Put these figures into an Excel spreadsheet (.xlsx).

Name the format and the structure you want — headings, a summary table, a 10-slide deck — and Copilot generates the file ready for you to refine.

Copilot in the Office Apps

In Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams, Copilot works right where you do. The key idea to grasp first: Copilot can write straight into the file you have open — not just answer in a side pane.

Editing Mode — Copilot writes into your open object

Inside an Office app, Copilot can do two things: chat with you in the side pane, or switch to Editing Mode and put content directly into the document, email, spreadsheet or deck in front of you.

Ask in chat

Copilot answers in the side pane. You read the result and copy what you need into your file.

Editing Mode

Copilot writes the content straight into the open object — drafting a Word document, filling Excel cells, building PowerPoint slides or composing an Outlook email.

Custom skills — teach Cowork a task once

A skill is a modular capability you hand a session so it performs one specific task reliably and repeatably. It isn’t more knowledge and it isn’t another tool — it’s a packaged procedure, with the assets and scripts that procedure needs travelling alongside it. The one-line distinction worth remembering: an agent is the “who”; a skill is the “how”. Add a skill when a task must be done the same way every single time — your report format, your naming convention, your review checklist.

1

From the Customize page

The guided route: open Customize in Cowork and describe the task. Good first skill: a document format you produce every week.

2

By asking in chat

Describe the procedure to Cowork in plain language and ask it to save the result as a reusable skill, then review what it wrote.

3

By hand, as a file

Author a SKILL.md file yourself and drop it in the skills folder — full control over the instructions and any scripts.

Where they live, and when they load. Custom skills are stored as SKILL.md files in your OneDrive at /Documents/Cowork/skills/ — up to 50 of them. Cowork discovers your skills at the start of each session, so a skill you add mid-session isn’t picked up until the next one. The same applies to plugins: removing one takes effect in the next session, never mid-flight. Skill scripts run in a temporary, isolated environment inside the Microsoft 365 service boundary, removed when the task finishes — and Microsoft doesn’t validate user-authored skills, so give a new skill’s output the same review you’d give a colleague’s first draft.

10 Tips & Tricks to Maximize Cowork

Grounded in Microsoft’s official Best practices for Copilot Cowork guidance plus the full feature set — blending the core best practices with ways to get more value and spend credits efficiently. A recurring theme: use M365 Copilot Chat (included in your seat) to plan and design, then deploy the finished task to Cowork (usage-billed) to execute — think and draft inexpensively, and spend credits only on the real work.

  1. Be specific, and give rich context up front. “Email Sarah about the Q2 review tomorrow at 2 PM” beats “Send that email.” Precise instructions cut re-work, wrong turns and wasted credits. Load everything in one pass — drag-and-drop files, add work context (files, people, emails, Teams chats/channels/meetings), or pull from OneDrive, SharePoint and Teams — up to 250,000 characters per message. Good context in one pass beats several clarifying round-trips.
  2. Right-size the request to the task. A quick draft is a light task; a 6-month data analysis is heavy. Ask for exactly the depth you need — don’t request a full deep-research report when a two-line summary would do.
  3. Batch related work into one session. Credit cost scales with context-loading and runtime, so fold several related asks into one well-scoped request (e.g. “read these emails and draft the brief and email the summary”). It reuses the loaded context instead of paying to rebuild it each time.
  4. Plan for complexity — and design it in Copilot Chat first. Allow extra time for heavy work (decks, cross-source research, large meetings) — complex ≠ broken, it’s just doing more steps. For anything non-trivial, use M365 Copilot Chat as your design studio: plan the approach, refine the exact prompt and preview the output there — Chat is included in your seat and draws no Cowork credits. Once it looks right, hand it to Cowork to run — so the credit-consuming session runs right the first time, not after several expensive retries.
  5. Draft in Markdown (.md) first, then convert. Ask Cowork for the first draft as a .md file — the native format for AI tools, the least expensive to generate and edit, and easiest to preview inline. When the content is right, convert it to the final artifact — Word, PowerPoint, Excel, PDF, HTML and more. You iterate inexpensively on the words, and spend on the polished format only once the substance is locked.
  6. Keep the conversation going responsibly. Long, sprawling sessions tend to cost more per turn — your accumulated context (files, history and past interactions) is carried and re-processed on every iteration, and context is a priced part of each task. Consolidate related work into one well-scoped session (tip 3), but start a fresh session for a genuinely new task rather than piling it onto a bloated one. Watch the climb with /cost, and stop refining once you hit diminishing returns.
  7. Build custom skills for tasks you repeat. Create reusable skills three ways — the guided Customize page, by asking Cowork in chat, or a SKILL.md file in /Documents/Cowork/skills/. Codify your standard formats once so every future run is consistent and faster. Up to 50 skills; share trusted ones with colleagues.
  8. Tap plugin skills from the Microsoft 365 App Store. Acquired plugins appear alongside built-in skills and activate automatically based on session context — extending Cowork with no manual wiring.
  9. Work with the full file lifecycle. Cowork reads and produces Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, images, code and more. Preview files inline (split view; Ctrl+F in PDFs), grab outputs from the side panel, or Download All as one zip (up to 50 files / 500 MB). Everything also lands in OneDrive → Documents/Cowork.
  10. Track your spend with /cost. Type /cost in the prompt box of any Cowork session to see the Copilot Credits it actually consumed. Run it periodically — and after a few representative light / medium / heavy tasks — to learn your real per-task costs and keep credit consumption in view as you work.

Agents & harnesses — what you're actually choosing

An LLM on its own is not an agent: it generates text and stops. What turns it into something that performs work is the harness — the runtime scaffolding around the model. The harness decides when to call the model, what to send it, how to read what comes back, and which tool to call next. It also manages conversation state and context, applies approval policies, and keeps a multi-step task moving.

Agent = LLM + Harness

Notice what that means: neither term is something you write. You select a model and you select a harness. What you author is the third thing — the design layer — and it has exactly three components.

I

Instructions

The agent's role, scope, tone and boundaries — and how it should call resources, fill tool inputs and respond.

K

Knowledge

The grounding sources it answers from — SharePoint, files, connectors, approved websites — so answers are anchored, not invented.

T

Tools

What it can do beyond talking — connectors, APIs, MCP servers, flows, and other agents it can invoke.

The design layer is built around the harness, never independently of it. The harness decides which of the three you can express, how richly, and whether some are available at all. So: choose the harness first, then design into it.

Considerations: Grounded Knowledge vs. Attached Cloud Files

When you hand Copilot documents to work from, there are two ways to do it — and they behave differently. Grounded knowledge is a knowledge source you wire into the agent at build time (for example, a SharePoint folder) — it lets Copilot find the relevant bits across many documents. Attached cloud files are files you attach to the prompt at runtime — they point Copilot straight at specific files so it reads them thoroughly. In short: grounded knowledge optimizes for relevance across a library; attached files optimize for completeness on the few you attach.

Grounded knowledge (build time) — use when…
  • You're searching a large document library
  • You're answering FAQ-style questions
  • You're discovering things — "which policy mentions X?", "what are the common themes?"
Attached cloud files (at the prompt) — use when…
  • Legal or policy review and validation
  • Exception analysis
  • "Make sure you don't miss anything" tasks on specific files

Skills — packaging work your agent repeats

Agent Builder gives your agent 8,000 characters of instructions. That is a real design budget, and it is the reason skills exist. A skill is a modular capability you add to an agent so it performs a specific task more reliably and repeatably — not more knowledge, and not another tool, but a packaged procedure with the assets and scripts that procedure needs travelling alongside it. An agent is the “who”; a skill is the “how”.

SourceCustom skills in declarative agents (preview) — Microsoft LearnModule 6 · 8 of 12
The anatomy of a skill

A skill is a file directory. Exactly one file is mandatory.

1

SKILL.md — required

YAML front matter carries the name and description — what the orchestrator reads to decide when the skill applies, so they're load-bearing, not labels. Below it, the procedure itself.

2

Resource files — optional

The assets the task needs: branded templates, KPI definitions, reference tables, schemas.

3

Scripts — optional

Code you control instead of code the model improvises — the same transformation, the same chart, every run.

A worked example from the documentation: a finance team's quarterly business review skill bundles the instructions for producing the review, scripts that transform the data and build the charts, and assets such as financial models, KPI definitions and branded slide templates.

Optimized context

Progressive disclosure — the model loads a skill's instructions and contents only when the task needs them, so context stays focused as your skill library grows.

Decompose complex guidance into modules: longer, more specific direction without degrading instruction-following or hitting the 8,000-character ceiling.

Reusable assets

Instructions, resources and scripts ship together as one integrable capability.

Scripts run in a secure sandbox, sensitivity labels on skill files are retained and honored, and files are stored in tenant-scoped SharePoint Embedded containers.

Does this change the harness? No. Agent Builder still produces declarative agents on the Copilot chat harness — custom skills are a new component inside the agent, not a migration to a different runtime. Copilot Studio — the maker harness, and when to graduate

Copilot Studio is where the other two harnesses live. The standard harness is the classic Copilot Studio most makers know: you author topics, prompts and paths, and the agent responds consistently to well-understood requests — an internal help desk that answers common questions and routes simple ones through a flow. The GitHub Copilot harness is the most capable option: it takes a goal, breaks it into steps, calls tools across connectors, knowledge, MCP and connected agents, adjusts when a step fails, natively creates and edits Word, Excel, PowerPoint and PDF, and supports skills and memory. It is generally available in Copilot Studio — you choose the harness when you create an agent — and usage is billed in Copilot Credits, including build and test.

Stay in Agent Builder when…

One person's job, answered from knowledge you can attach or reach through Work IQ; one grounding pass plus one tool call produces a complete answer; the deliverable is a generated file; publishing is to your team.

Graduate to Copilot Studio when…

You hit the walls: memory across a task, reasoning loops, transactions against a line-of-business system, editing an existing document in place, autonomous or event-triggered runs, multi-user orchestration, or reach outside the tenant.

Reach for the lightest tool that does the job. The same three tiers that power Copilot also decide where you build an agent and what the agent you build can do — you only graduate to Copilot Studio when an agent needs Tier-3 reach.

Always check these capabilities and boundaries against the latest Microsoft Learn release every time you do solutioning.
