Prompting Like a Pro

A prompt is simply how you ask Copilot to do something — create, summarize, edit, transform or catch you up. Talk to it like a capable assistant: plain, clear language, with enough context to do the job well.

From vague to valuable

The same task — preparing for an AI Engineer interview — gets stronger as you add ingredients.

Good

"Help me prepare for an upcoming candidate interview for a new AI Engineer role in our IT department by creating a list of questions."

Better

"I'm an HR Manager preparing for a candidate interview for our new AI Engineer role in IT. Please prepare a list of appropriate questions so I look prepared during the interview."

Best

"I'm an HR Manager preparing for a candidate interview for our new AI Engineer role in IT. Please review the candidate's resume here: /[resume] and the position detail here: /[position doc] and give me a comprehensive list of questions to assess the candidate's fit for the position."

Extending the purpose and keeping the conversation
Extend the purpose

Before you hit send, think about what you'll actually do with the output — then ask Copilot to do the bigger job.

1 Why am I using this prompt?
2 What will I do with the output — send it on, reshape it?
3 Can I ask Copilot to take on the bigger task?
Just the task

"Recap the /[meeting] with a table of action items, owners and due dates."

The bigger job

"Write a follow-up email to the /[meeting] attendees with a table of decisions made, a table of actions and owners, and a list of topics for the next meeting."

Keep the conversation going

Copilot gets better as you follow up. Start broad, then refine — just like working with a colleague.

Generating ideas

Lead with a broad request, then add specifics.

Insightful meetings

Ask for a recap, then "what should I know?"

Storytelling

Ask for a draft, then guide it with details.

Deeper insights

Summarize a file, then ask follow-up questions.

Translation

Translate, then ask for tone or a regional dialect.

Technical problems

State the problem, then narrow it down step by step.

Seven prompting best practices
1

Length & clarity matter

Too short gives generic answers; too long and Copilot may miss instructions. Be clear and complete.

2

Break it into steps

Outline first, then expand each section, then add statistics and detail.

3

Use a persona

Tell Copilot the role to adopt for expert, tailored responses.

4

Ask what it needs

Not sure what to include? Ask Copilot what details it needs to succeed.

5

Think holistically

Look at whole processes — risk, comms, customer experience — not just single tasks.

6

Be curious

Experiment with different structures. Curiosity reveals new capabilities.

7

Test & refine

Iterate. Adjust prompts based on the results you get.

Try it yourself — prompt face-off

STEP 1

Run the weak prompt

In Word, open Copilot and ask: "Write a job description for a senior program manager." Notice how generic it is.

STEP 2

Run the strong prompt

Now ask for a job description for a senior program manager focused on Azure cloud — concise, max two pages, with responsibilities and qualifications, using your standard specs.

STEP 3

Compare

Which draft is richer and more usable? Spot the Goal, Context, Source and Expectations you added.

Fun fact! — ask for the output format you want

Copilot doesn't only answer in chat — you can ask it to deliver the result as a ready-to-edit file in the format you need. Just name the format in your prompt and Copilot builds the document, deck, workbook or page for you.

Summarize this report as a Word document (.docx).
Turn these notes into a PowerPoint deck (.pptx).
Put these figures into an Excel spreadsheet (.xlsx).
Give me this as a single self-contained HTML page (.html).

Name the format and the structure you want — headings, a summary table, a 10-slide deck — and Copilot generates the file ready for you to refine.

Power move! — trigger Work IQ to act in your other apps

Work IQ doesn't only read across your Microsoft 365 — it can also act. Ask Copilot Chat to draft an email or set up a meeting and it pulls that app's action right into the chat: an Outlook compose card or a scheduling pop-up appears inline, so you review and confirm the send without ever leaving the M365 Copilot Chat UX.

Draft an email — "Send the summary above to a colleague" opens an Outlook compose card here in chat.
Schedule a meeting — "Find 30 minutes with the project team next week and send the invite."
Reply in place — "Reply to the last email accepting the review and proposing Thursday."
Post to Teams — "Send these action items to our project channel."

Copilot drafts the email or meeting inside the chat — you review the pop-up, adjust it, then send or schedule. The action runs in Outlook, your calendar or Teams, but you never leave Copilot.
