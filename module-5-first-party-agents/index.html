First-Party Agents

Beyond chat, Microsoft Copilot ships with ready-made first-party agents — built by Microsoft and available right inside Copilot. No building required: open one and go.

5.1 What are first-party agents — and where to find them

First-party agents are specialized assistants built by Microsoft for specific jobs — research, data analysis, and more. You don't build them; you just open one and go. Find them all in Plugins: from the navigation pane, choose Plugins (labelled Agents & Skills until Plugins reaches your tenant).

Plugins — from October 2026

Plugins extend what Copilot can do by bringing skills, connectors and other capabilities together in one package. A new plugin registry gathers Microsoft, partner and custom-built plugins into one catalog that IT approves and manages centrally.

Plugins replaces AgentsIn the Copilot app navigation pane, Plugins replaces the standalone Agents (Agents & Skills) entry in Chat and the Customize entry in Cowork.
Pin what you useResearcher and Analyst are no longer pinned by default — open them from Plugins and pin them yourself. Your admin can pre-pin them for everyone.
When it arrivesRolling out to the Frontier program from October 2026, together with the new Home. Until then, agents stay under Agents & Skills.

5.2 Word, Excel & PowerPoint Agents

These three agents are installed for you by Microsoft and live in the Copilot app — open one from the Tools menu or the All agents list in the sidebar. Tell it what you need in plain language and it creates a brand-new file for you, saved straight to your OneDrive. They're different from Edit with Copilot: rather than working inside a file you already have open, they build the document, workbook or deck from your prompt.

Premium only. These agents need a paid Microsoft Copilot license — they aren’t available to Copilot Chat (Basic) users. They ground in your work content through Work IQ — the files, emails, meetings and sites you already have permission to see. They run on Anthropic models: if you can’t see or open them, your admin may not have enabled Anthropic.

One thing to know before you reach for them: these three are builders first. They are specialized in turning a prompt into a finished file — a document, a deck, a workbook — and the Excel Agent will also analyze the data it builds from, producing charts and year-to-date summaries. When the job is deep analysis of a dataset rather than producing a file, reach for the Analyst agent below.

Word Agent

A finished document from a brief — reports, proposals, policies, briefing notes. Give it the audience and the length.

PowerPoint Agent

A designed deck from a prompt or a source document — it drafts the slides, organizes the flow and applies the design.

Excel Agent

A working spreadsheet from a description — it builds the table, adds the formulas and sets up the structure.

Example Use Case Prompts

Draft a two-page onboarding policy for new hires — purpose, scope, the five steps, and a checklist table at the end.
Write a one-page project proposal for a customer onboarding portal: goals, scope, timeline and how we’ll measure success.
Build a 10-slide overview introducing Microsoft Copilot to new employees — what it is, where to find it, three use cases, next steps.
Turn /Q3 Proposal.docx into a 6-slide executive deck, ending with a risks-and-mitigations slide.
Build a project budget tracker with columns for item, owner, planned cost, actual cost and variance, plus a summary row.
Create a 12-month cash-flow template with formulas for monthly totals and a running balance.

Every file these agents create is saved to your OneDrive, inside your organization's tenant — so it stays under the same security, sensitivity-label and compliance rules as the rest of your work.

5.3 Researcher & Analyst
Researcher

Researcher

The agent you hand a question you can’t answer in one search — it goes away, reads widely, and comes back with a brief you could put in front of your leadership.

Reaches for
The open web and your work content — files, emails, meetings, chats
Trades
Speed for depth — minutes of multi-step reasoning, not a one-shot reply
Gives you back
A structured, source-cited report you can verify claim by claim

Before you run it. Researcher allows up to 25 queries per user per month — save it for high-stakes questions. Simple queries finish in under five minutes; highly complex ones take 10 to 45 minutes. Choose the model and mode in the model picker at the top right (with @Researcher in a normal chat, it defaults to Auto).

Researcher is built for depth over speed. It reasons through a question across several steps, drawing on both the web and your work content — files, emails, meetings and chats you have permission to see — and returns a comprehensive, source-cited report rather than a chat answer. It deliberately takes longer than a normal reply because it is doing multi-step analysis, checking as it goes, and assembling citations you can verify. Use it when the cost of being wrong is high and you need to show your working: a market landscape, a competitor picture, a decision brief. Give it a clear scope and say whether it should look at your work content, the web, or both.

Example Use Case Prompt

Research the managed-services market in Southeast Asia for our FY27 plan: size the top five providers, summarize how each one positions, fold in what my emails and meetings say about the two we’ve competed with this year, and give me a cited brief with a table of strengths and gaps.

Analyst

Analyst

A data scientist you can hand a messy export to — it works the numbers step by step, shows the code it ran, and tells you what the data is actually saying.

Reaches for
The file you give it — spreadsheets, exports, CSVs, raw and unpolished
Trades
A formula for real analysis — segments, correlations, forecasts
Gives you back
Charts, tables and a plain-language read — with the Python it ran

Analyst works like a data scientist on your team. It reasons through a dataset step by step — and runs Python when a question calls for it — to surface trends, comparisons and anomalies, then explains its thinking and shows the visuals behind it. You can watch the code it runs and check its work, which is the point: the answer comes with its reasoning attached. Reach for it when the question is bigger than a formula — segments, correlations, forecasts, or “what is actually going on in this export?” — and hand it the file rather than a description of the file.
