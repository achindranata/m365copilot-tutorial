First-Party Agents

Beyond chat, Microsoft 365 Copilot ships with ready-made first-party agents — built by Microsoft and available right inside Copilot. No building required: open one and go.

What are first-party agents — and where to find them

First-party agents are specialized assistants built by Microsoft for specific jobs — research, data analysis, and more. You don't build them; you just open one and go. Find them all in the Agent Store: from the sidebar, choose Agents.

Word, Excel & PowerPoint Agents

These three agents are installed for you by Microsoft and live in the Copilot app — open one from the Tools menu or the All agents list in the sidebar. Tell it what you need in plain language and it creates a brand-new file for you, saved straight to your OneDrive. They're different from Edit with Copilot: rather than working inside a file you already have open, they build the document, workbook or deck from your prompt.

Copilot Chat (Basic)M365 Copilot (Premium)

Where they get their information. On Copilot Chat (Basic) the agents draw on the web and files you attach. With Microsoft 365 Copilot (Premium) they also ground in your work content through Work IQ — the files, emails, meetings and sites you already have permission to see.

One thing to know before you reach for them: these three are builders first. They are specialized in turning a prompt into a finished file — a document, a deck, a workbook — and the Excel Agent will also analyze the data it builds from, producing charts and year-to-date summaries. When the job is deep analysis of a dataset rather than producing a file, reach for the Analyst agent below.

Word Agent

A finished document from a brief — reports, proposals, policies, briefing notes. Give it the audience and the length.

PowerPoint Agent

A designed deck from a prompt or a source document — it drafts the slides, organizes the flow and applies the design.

Excel Agent

A working spreadsheet from a description — it builds the table, adds the formulas and sets up the structure.

Example Use Case Prompts

Draft a two-page onboarding policy for new hires — purpose, scope, the five steps, and a checklist table at the end.
Write a one-page project proposal for a customer onboarding portal: goals, scope, timeline and how we’ll measure success.
Build a 10-slide overview introducing Microsoft 365 Copilot to new employees — what it is, where to find it, three use cases, next steps.
Turn /Q3 Proposal.docx into a 6-slide executive deck, ending with a risks-and-mitigations slide.
Build a project budget tracker with columns for item, owner, planned cost, actual cost and variance, plus a summary row.
Create a 12-month cash-flow template with formulas for monthly totals and a running balance.

Every file these agents create is saved to your OneDrive, inside your organization's tenant — so it stays under the same security, sensitivity-label and compliance rules as the rest of your work.

Researcher & Analyst
Researcher

Researcher

The agent you hand a question you can’t answer in one search — it goes away, reads widely, and comes back with a brief you could put in front of your leadership.

Reaches for
The open web and your work content — files, emails, meetings, chats
Trades
Speed for depth — minutes of multi-step reasoning, not a one-shot reply
Gives you back
A structured, source-cited report you can verify claim by claim

Researcher is built for depth over speed. It reasons through a question across several steps, drawing on both the web and your work content — files, emails, meetings and chats you have permission to see — and returns a comprehensive, source-cited report rather than a chat answer. It deliberately takes longer than a normal reply because it is doing multi-step analysis, checking as it goes, and assembling citations you can verify. Use it when the cost of being wrong is high and you need to show your working: a market landscape, a competitor picture, a decision brief. Give it a clear scope and say whether it should look at your work content, the web, or both.

Example Use Case Prompt

Research the managed-services market in Southeast Asia for our FY27 plan: size the top five providers, summarize how each one positions, fold in what my emails and meetings say about the two we’ve competed with this year, and give me a cited brief with a table of strengths and gaps.

Analyst

Analyst

A data scientist you can hand a messy export to — it works the numbers step by step, shows the code it ran, and tells you what the data is actually saying.

Reaches for
The file you give it — spreadsheets, exports, CSVs, raw and unpolished
Trades
A formula for real analysis — segments, correlations, forecasts
Gives you back
Charts, tables and a plain-language read — with the Python it ran

Analyst works like a data scientist on your team. It reasons through a dataset step by step — and runs Python when a question calls for it — to surface trends, comparisons and anomalies, then explains its thinking and shows the visuals behind it. You can watch the code it runs and check its work, which is the point: the answer comes with its reasoning attached. Reach for it when the question is bigger than a formula — segments, correlations, forecasts, or “what is actually going on in this export?” — and hand it the file rather than a description of the file.

M365 Copilot (Premium)
Cowork

Cowork

The agent you hand an outcome rather than a question — it plans the steps, works across your apps for as long as the job takes, and checks with you before anything leaves the building.

Reaches for
Your Outlook, Teams, files and data — grounded in Work IQ, inside your own permissions
Trades
A single reply for a finished outcome — minutes to hours, running on its own
Gives you back
Real files in your OneDrive, a task you can reopen, and an approval gate before it sends

Cowork is the agentic side of Microsoft 365 Copilot — you describe an outcome and it plans and carries out the steps for you, across your apps. You don’t build it: it’s a ready-made agent, on the Cowork tab in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app.

How it’s designed. Cowork is a prebuilt, autonomous agent that takes action, not just chats. Grounded in your emails, meetings, chats, files and data via Work IQ, it works through a multi-step job across Outlook, Teams, Word, Excel and PowerPoint. Where Copilot Chat answers in a single turn, Cowork works over minutes to hours — pausing for your approval before every send, post or schedule, running in a secure, isolated environment, and acting only within your own permissions. Getting access takes two things, not one: an active Microsoft 365 Copilot license and usage-based (Cowork) billing enabled — the spending policy is an access control, not just a budget.

Copilot Chat vs Cowork — which do you use? Use Copilot Chat to think, draft and answer in the moment; reach for Cowork when you want work carried out across several apps and steps.

Copilot Chat — think & draft

Single-step, single session · seconds to minutes · you act on the output.

  • Quick draft, answer or insight
  • Reason over one attached file
  • Fast, focused, single task
Cowork — get it done

Multi-step, across apps · minutes to hours · autonomous, you approve each action.

  • Inbox & calendar clean-up
  • Project launches & meeting prep
  • Recurring, scheduled work

Reach for Cowork when…

  • the task is multi-step and can run largely on its own (e.g. research → draft → compile → follow-up), not a single prompt;
  • it should work over your own Microsoft 365 data (Work IQ) and take real actions — send mail, post in Teams, build docs, manage files — each behind an approval;
  • you want a ready-made agent you can extend with up to 50 custom skills and Microsoft 365 App Store plugins & connectors, rather than building one in Agent Builder or Copilot Studio;
  • it should run on a schedule — recurring prompts such as a daily briefing or a follow-up sweep;
  • the work stays within one person’s identity and permissions, with you reviewing the result.

Custom skills — teach Cowork a task once

A skill is a modular capability you hand a session so it performs one specific task reliably and repeatably. It isn’t more knowledge and it isn’t another tool — it’s a packaged procedure, with the assets and scripts that procedure needs travelling alongside it. The one-line distinction worth remembering: an agent is the “who”; a skill is the “how”. Add a skill when a task must be done the same way every single time — your report format, your naming convention, your review checklist.

1

From the Customize page

The guided route: open Customize in Cowork and describe the task. Good first skill: a document format you produce every week.

2

By asking in chat

Describe the procedure to Cowork in plain language and ask it to save the result as a reusable skill, then review what it wrote.

3

By hand, as a file

Author a SKILL.md file yourself and drop it in the skills folder — full control over the instructions and any scripts.

Where they live, and when they load. Custom skills are stored as SKILL.md files in your OneDrive at /Documents/Cowork/skills/ — up to 50 of them. Cowork discovers your skills at the start of each session, so a skill you add mid-session isn’t picked up until the next one. The same applies to plugins: removing one takes effect in the next session, never mid-flight. Skill scripts run in a temporary, isolated environment inside the Microsoft 365 service boundary, removed when the task finishes — and Microsoft doesn’t validate user-authored skills, so give a new skill’s output the same review you’d give a colleague’s first draft.

10 Tips & Tricks to Maximize Cowork

Grounded in Microsoft’s official Best practices for Copilot Cowork guidance plus the full feature set — blending the core best practices with ways to get more value and spend credits efficiently. A recurring theme: use M365 Copilot Chat (included in your seat) to plan and design, then deploy the finished task to Cowork (usage-billed) to execute — think and draft inexpensively, and spend credits only on the real work.

  1. Be specific, and give rich context up front. “Email Sarah about the Q2 review tomorrow at 2 PM” beats “Send that email.” Precise instructions cut re-work, wrong turns and wasted credits. Load everything in one pass — drag-and-drop files, add work context (files, people, emails, Teams chats/channels/meetings), or pull from OneDrive, SharePoint and Teams — up to 250,000 characters per message. Good context in one pass beats several clarifying round-trips.
  2. Right-size the request to the task. A quick draft is a light task; a 6-month data analysis is heavy. Ask for exactly the depth you need — don’t request a full deep-research report when a two-line summary would do.
  3. Batch related work into one session. Credit cost scales with context-loading and runtime, so fold several related asks into one well-scoped request (e.g. “read these emails and draft the brief and email the summary”). It reuses the loaded context instead of paying to rebuild it each time.
  4. Plan for complexity — and design it in Copilot Chat first. Allow extra time for heavy work (decks, cross-source research, large meetings) — complex ≠ broken, it’s just doing more steps. For anything non-trivial, use M365 Copilot Chat as your design studio: plan the approach, refine the exact prompt and preview the output there — Chat is included in your seat and draws no Cowork credits. Once it looks right, hand it to Cowork to run — so the credit-consuming session runs right the first time, not after several expensive retries.
  5. Draft in Markdown (.md) first, then convert. Ask Cowork for the first draft as a .md file — the native format for AI tools, the least expensive to generate and edit, and easiest to preview inline. When the content is right, convert it to the final artifact — Word, PowerPoint, Excel, PDF, HTML and more. You iterate inexpensively on the words, and spend on the polished format only once the substance is locked.
  6. Keep the conversation going responsibly. Long, sprawling sessions tend to cost more per turn — your accumulated context (files, history and past interactions) is carried and re-processed on every iteration, and context is a priced part of each task. Consolidate related work into one well-scoped session (tip 3), but start a fresh session for a genuinely new task rather than piling it onto a bloated one. Watch the climb with /cost, and stop refining once you hit diminishing returns.
  7. Build custom skills for tasks you repeat. Create reusable skills three ways — the guided Customize page, by asking Cowork in chat, or a SKILL.md file in /Documents/Cowork/skills/. Codify your standard formats once so every future run is consistent and faster. Up to 50 skills; share trusted ones with colleagues.
  8. Tap plugin skills from the Microsoft 365 App Store. Acquired plugins appear alongside built-in skills and activate automatically based on session context — extending Cowork with no manual wiring.
  9. Work with the full file lifecycle. Cowork reads and produces Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, images, code and more. Preview files inline (split view; Ctrl+F in PDFs), grab outputs from the side panel, or Download All as one zip (up to 50 files / 500 MB). Everything also lands in OneDrive → Documents/Cowork.
  10. Track your spend with /cost. Type /cost in the prompt box of any Cowork session to see the Copilot Credits it actually consumed. Run it periodically — and after a few representative light / medium / heavy tasks — to learn your real per-task costs and keep credit consumption in view as you work.

Special tip — Design in Chat, deploy to Cowork

Make “design in Chat → deploy to Cowork” your default loop — the single biggest credit-saver, and the engine behind tip 4. For any important, ambiguous or repeated task, work across two surfaces:

Design in Chat

Plan the steps, draft and tighten the prompt, and preview the deliverable’s content and structure — all within your included seat at no cost in Cowork Copilot Credits.

→
Deploy in Cowork

Paste the finished prompt into a Cowork session to run the real multi-tool, multi-source execution — sending, scheduling and building files across your data.

Iterate in Chat (included in your Premium plan) until the prompt is right, then let Cowork do the credit-consuming work once. Promote winning prompts into a custom skill (tip 7) or a recurring scheduled prompt so a single design keeps paying off.
